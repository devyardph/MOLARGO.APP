What each person at the practice does, in the order they do it — and what the software will and will not let them do.

1  Before anyone signs in

What Molargo is

One patient record, with the diary, the chart, billing, prescribing and the worklists hanging off it. The database is a file on the machine, so the surgery keeps working when the connection does not.

What it is not

Stated plainly, because a manual that describes features the software lacks sends people hunting for buttons that were never there.

Not builtWhat that means on the day
Online booking, patient portalEvery appointment is made by a person, in the diary.
Payment gatewayNo card is ever taken. Every payment recorded is money that arrived somewhere else.
Health fund claimingClaims are recorded and tracked. Submitting them needs HICAPS or a fund's own channel.
Electronic prescriptionsNo eRx token. The printed, signed sheet is the script.
Syncing between machinesEach installation is its own database. Nothing merges.
Campaign sendingTemplates and audiences exist; the send does not.
Anything on a timerReminders go when somebody presses the button. Nothing runs unattended.

Before you go live

Password resets and two-step codes are emailed through the practice's own mail account, set up at Admin → Settings. Until that exists nobody can reset their own password — somebody with staff access has to set a new one on their record. Two-step sign-in cannot be switched on at all without it.

Every sign-in attempt, successful or not, is written to Admin → Audit log.

2  How access really works

Most practice software gates screens by job title: a receptionist sees the receptionist's menu. Molargo does not work that way, and knowing this before you set up your staff will save you an argument later.

Three separate things decide what somebody can do.

The thingWhat it decidesWhat it does not decide
Clinic
tenant		 Which practice's records exist for you at all. Enforced in the database, not the screen. Nothing within your own practice.
Role
Dentist, Hygienist, Administration…		 Whether you can be booked into a chair, whether you count as a billable clinician seat, and — in the places that check — whether you are answerable for a clinical record. Which menus you see. No role hides a section, with one exception (3.7).
Permissions
five flags		 Every administrative write in the app: staff, pricing, sites, stock costs, settings. Clinical work. A dentist with no permissions still charts, prescribes and bills.
Owner
one person		 Everything, implicitly — plus the two things no permission can grant. —
The practical consequence

A receptionist account can open Charting and look at a tooth chart. Nothing in the software stops them. What stops them is that they are not bookable, they hold no administrative permission, and a certificate will refuse to issue in their name because they have no licence number on file. Molargo's confidentiality boundary is the clinic, not the job title — and every record opened is written to the audit log, which is how "who looked at this" gets answered.

If you need a screen-level boundary between front desk and surgery, it does not exist yet. Say so to your team rather than assuming it.

An assistant is excluded from clinical authorship despite working chairside: they assist with treatment but do not diagnose, prescribe or certify, and the record has to name whoever is answerable for the decision.

Assistants and reception being free is not generosity. Charging per front-desk login is what makes a practice share one reception account, and a shared account makes the audit log worthless.

2.2  The five permissions

Granted per person at Admin → Users. A new account gets none of them — clinical work only, nothing administrative.

PermissionUnlocksWorth knowing
Manage staff Admin → Users and Roles: adding people, editing them, setting passwords The most powerful of the five by some distance. Anyone who can set another person's password can sign in as them. Grant it last.
Manage pricing The item catalogue's editor: fees, per-site prices, adding and withdrawing items Editing only. Reading the catalogue is open to everyone — the front desk quotes from it all day.
Manage sites Admin → Sites: locations, their details, opening hours, and the diary's chairs Opening hours set here are what the diary refuses bookings against.
Manage inventory Stock cost prices, suppliers, purchase orders Not receiving stock or marking it used — that is surgery and front-desk work and stays open to all. This gates the money side.
Manage settings Admin → Settings: the mail account, notification rules, printers Hands over the ability to send email as the practice. The app password is stored in plain text in the database file.

Holding any one of manage staff, manage sites or manage settings puts Admin in the top bar. Pricing and inventory do not: those are edited on Billing and Inventory, outside the Admin section, so holding them is no reason to show an Admin tab whose every pane would refuse.

2.3  What only the owner can do

Owner-onlyWhy it is not a grantable permission
Change who owns the practice, and who holds which permissions If it were grantable, whoever held it could grant themselves every other permission and then ownership — which would make the rest of this section decoration.
Export the whole database The file is a complete, unencrypted copy of every patient record the practice holds. Whoever can produce one can walk out with the practice.
There is no way back in

There is no vendor override and no master password. The owner's permissions are computed, not stored, precisely so an owner cannot be left holding a partial set and locked out of their own practice. Keep the owner account recoverable: a working mail account, and a second person with manage staff who could set a password if the owner is hit by a bus.

3  The day, by role

Everyone lands on the front desk. What differs is where they go next, and which of the day's records they are the one to create.

3.1  Receptionist / front desk

Role: Administration  ·  Permissions: usually none  ·  Free seat

The busiest account in the building, and the one that touches every transaction before and after the clinician does.

Cancelled and Failed to attend sit outside the ladder and a stray tap cannot walk them back into it. A no-show is tracked separately from a cancellation because it costs the practice the slot and feeds the patient's missed-appointment count.

Taking a booking on the phone

  1. Search the patient from the top bar, or Patients → New patient if they are new.
  2. Diary → New appointment. Patient, type, provider, chair, time.
  3. Read the pre-booking panel on the right before you save: medical alerts, outstanding lab work, account balance, missed-appointment history.
  4. Tick Tell the patient — email, text, or both. Each box explains itself when it cannot be used.
  5. Save. What went out is written to the patient's communication log, including anything that failed.

The form warns rather than blocks for a double-booking — sometimes that is deliberate — but it refuses a day the practice is closed.

Taking money

Billing → Invoices, raise from the fee catalogue, record the payment against it. No card is taken anywhere in this app: you are recording money that arrived by terminal, transfer or cash. Billing → End of day is the reconciliation, Debtors the chase list.

What a receptionist cannot do

not bookable They cannot appear as the provider on an appointment. no certificate A certificate will not issue in their name — it needs a licence number on the record. no admin With no permissions, Admin is absent from the top bar.

3.2  Dentist

Role: Dentist  ·  Permissions: usually none  ·  Billable clinician seat

A dentist with no permissions at all can do every piece of clinical and billing work below. Permissions are about administering the practice, not treating patients.

What signing a note means here

A signed clinical note is the record. Later changes are additions, not edits — the original text stays. A note that could be quietly rewritten is a note nobody can rely on in a complaint, which is the only moment it really matters.

The rest of a dentist's screens

ScreenWhat they do there

Before issuing a certificate

The clinician must have a licence number on their staff record. Without one the issue is refused — a certificate signed by somebody with no licence number is not a document an employer can rely on, and issuing one puts the practice's name to a claim it cannot support. Set it at Admin → Users, which usually means asking the owner or practice manager.

3.3  Hygienist, oral health therapist, dental therapist

Role: as registered  ·  Permissions: usually none  ·  Billable clinician seat

The same flow as 3.2, with the same authority to chart, note, prescribe and certify within their own registration. Molargo does not police scope of practice for you — it records who was answerable, and your registration decides what you may do. Two practical differences:

  • Recalls are their engine. Scale & polish carries a six-month recall interval, so completing a hygiene visit is what puts the patient back on the worklist. See 4.1.
  • The patient's own interval wins. A periodontal patient on three months does not revert to six because they came in for a routine clean.

3.4  Dental assistant

Role: Dental assistant  ·  Permissions: usually none  ·  Free seat

Chairside support. Not bookable, not answerable for clinical records, not a paid seat.

  • Inventory → Stock — use and adjust, so what came off the shelf is recorded as it happens.
  • Inventory → Sterilisation — running and releasing loads, and tying a load to the patients it was used on.
  • Inventory → Lab — cases out and back.
  • Preparing the record: opening the chart, checking consent is signed, seeing the day's list.

Recording stock use and receiving deliveries is deliberately open to everyone. What needs the manage inventory permission is the money side — cost prices, suppliers and purchase orders.

3.5  Practice manager

Role: Administration  ·  Permissions: staff, sites, settings, pricing, inventory  ·  Free seat

Everything in 3.1, plus the Admin section. This is the account that sets the practice up and keeps it running.

The split at step 5

A practice manager with manage staff can add people, edit them and set passwords. They cannot change who holds which permissions, and cannot make anyone the owner — that is checked against the stored record rather than what is being saved, so it cannot be smuggled through an edit. Adding a colleague is not a route to granting yourself the practice.

The rest of the month

  • Admin → Plan — what the subscription costs, seats and sites counted, and the switch for text messaging.
  • Admin → Audit log — searchable, paged, and it records views as well as changes.
  • Billing → Item catalogue — the fees, with per-site prices where they differ.
  • Inventory → Orders — purchase orders, recorded rather than sent.
  • Admin → Data — the local database, its size and its backups.

3.6  Practice owner

Role: whatever they practise as  ·  Permissions: all, implicitly  ·  Billable if clinical

Everything above, plus the two things no permission grants: changing who owns the practice and who administers what, and exporting the whole database.

The owner is created by signup and cannot be left half-privileged. Their permissions are computed rather than stored, so a stray click — or a permission added to the software after their account was made — cannot lock them out of their own practice. There is nobody who could let them back in.

Owner's monthly ten minutes

  1. Reports → Overview — production, and where it came from.
  2. Billing → Debtors — what is owed and how old it is.
  3. Admin → Plan — seats counted from active clinical staff, so a departure that was never deactivated is money.
  4. Admin → Audit log — who has been looking at what.
  5. Admin → Data — take a backup off the machine. It is the only copy.

3.7  Vendor operator (super admin)

Role: Super admin  ·  Belongs to the vendor, not to any clinic

This is the only role in Molargo that actually restricts anything. A super admin sees a different top bar entirely — Clinics, Plans, Billing, SMS, Email, Users, Help — and none of the clinical sections, because the vendor's own tenant has no patients, no diary and no invoices.

A practice cannot grant this role: its own Users screen does not offer it, and accounts holding it sit outside the practice's records altogether.

Vendor screenWhat it is for
Platform → ClinicsEvery subscribing practice, its plan and its state.
Platform → PlansThe plans on sale, per country, with prices and included text allowances.
Platform → BillingRaising monthly subscription charges, including the previous month's text usage.
Platform → SMSThe gateway per country, its rate, its credit limit, and usage across all subscribers.
Platform → EmailThe vendor's own sending account — the one thing that can reach somebody before their clinic exists, which is what a signup code has to do.
Platform → HelpThe knowledge base every practice reads. Edited here, so a correction needs no release.

4  Transactions end to end

The flows below cross more than one pair of hands. Each lane is who does that step; the numbers run in order down the page.

4.1  New patient → first visit → invoice → recall

The spine of the system. Everything else hangs off it.

Front desk

1Create the patient. Patients → New patient. The form flags likely duplicates as you type — same name, same date of birth, same number — because a second record for one person splits their history in half.

2Book the appointment. Type, provider, chair, time. Read the pre-booking panel. Tick Tell the patient.
System

3Writes the confirmation to the patient's communication log — including a message that failed, because that log is the answer to "were they told".
Front desk

4The day before: Diary → Reminders → Send what is due. Running it twice is harmless — an attempt already made is never repeated, so an interrupted run can simply be run again.

5On arrival: advance the status to Checked in, then Seated.
Clinician

6Medical history — taken or confirmed. The questionnaire ships with the practice.

7Treat and chart. Odontogram or perio, per tooth and per surface.

8Write and sign the note. After signing, changes are additions.

9Advance the appointment to Completed.
System

10Completing a visit creates or rolls forward a recall, if the appointment type carries an interval. Exam & clean and Scale & polish do; a crown fit, an extraction and a root canal do not — those finish a course of treatment rather than starting the next one.
Front desk

11Raise the invoice from the fee catalogue and record the payment. The ledger is the truth; the total on the invoice follows it.

12Book the next visit while they are standing there. Booking at or near the recall's due date marks it Booked and takes it off the worklist.
Three rules worth knowing about recalls

One per patient. Finishing another visit rolls the existing recall forward rather than adding a second — two rows would be two people ringing the same patient in the same week.

An early booking does not clear it. Somebody coming in next week for a broken tooth has not had their check-up.

A patient who has declined recalls is never put back on the list by attending.

4.2  Treatment plan → presentation → consent → treatment

Clinician

1Start from the chart. The findings already recorded become the plan's starting items, rather than being retyped.

2Build it into visits — what happens on the first appointment, what on the second. Fees come from the catalogue.

3Present it chairside. The presentation screen shows the plan and its alternatives as the patient sees them.
Patient

4Accepts, or declines. Acceptance records who agreed and in what capacity — Self, Parent, Guardian — plus a signature where a tablet captured one. Consent for a child is given by somebody else, and a record that does not say who is not evidence.
Front desk

5Book the visits the accepted plan calls for. Each booking is linked back to the plan visit it covers.
System

6Booking a plan visit raises the consent forms its procedures need. Anything still unsigned is flagged on the arrivals list, so the front desk sees it before the patient sits down.
Clinician

7Consent signed, then treat. A visit whose procedures need no separate consent says so, rather than showing an empty list that looks like a loading failure.

4.3  A cancellation, and filling the gap

1. Patient cancelsFront desk sets the appointment Cancelled
→
2. Gap appearsFront desk counts runs of 15 min or more between bookings
→
3. WaitlistOrdered urgent first, then whoever has not been offered anything yet
→
4. Ring themLog offer · Booked · Remove
→
5. Slot refilledThe row is kept either way

Getting somebody onto the short-notice list takes seconds: Diary → Waitlist → Add someone. Everything except the patient is optional, because whoever is taking a cancellation call has thirty seconds.

Three buttons per row, because an offered slot has three outcomes. Log offer records that you rang and leaves them on the list. Booked and Remove take them off — and the row is kept either way, so "you never call me" has an answer.

Offers are made by hand. Automatic offers and first-reply-wins would need a messaging gateway racing replies, which is not built.

What the check is, and is not

The interaction screen is written by hand against the medicines in the formulary — it is not a pharmacological database, and the prescribing screen says so on the screen. Treat it as a prompt, not an authority.

Nothing is transmitted. An eRx token needs a prescription-exchange gateway, which is not built.

4.5  Medical certificate

Clinician

1Draft it. Rx & referrals → Certificates. Set the days unfit, and — if it is not today — the day they are unfit from. A patient seen late on a Friday is often unfit from Monday. Five days is the cap; beyond that they need their own doctor.

2Edit the wording while it is a draft — "light duties only", "unfit for heavy lifting". Keep the treatment out of it: an employer has no right to the clinical detail, which is why the standard wording says "dental treatment" and stops.

3Issue. Refused if the clinician has no licence number on file.

4Sign and print. A certificate printed before it is issued carries a visible "Draft — not issued" band.
System

5Once issued, the wording is fixed. Rewriting a document the patient already has would change what the practice said after it said it. Draft a new one instead.

6An issued certificate nobody signed is flagged in the list — an unsigned sheet is one an employer has no reason to accept.

Deleting a certificate takes it off the list, not out of the record: the wording is kept, and the audit log names who removed it and what it covered.

4.6  Month end: texts, usage and the subscription charge

Practice

1Texts go out through the month — reminders, recalls, booking confirmations. Admin → Plan shows the rate, how many have gone this month and what they come to.
System

2Counts only messages that actually went. Queued, failed and suppressed ones are not charged for.
Vendor

3Platform → Billing raises the month's charges: the plan, plus sites beyond the base, plus clinician seats beyond the allowance.

4Adds last month's text usage above the included allowance — a month is billed once it is finished and the total cannot move.
Nobody

5Nothing collects money. There is no invoice, no payment gateway and no renewal date. Every Paid or Failed on a charge was recorded by a person after money did or did not arrive somewhere else.

5  Where the system stops

Every line here is a deliberate absence, not a defect. Knowing them is what stops somebody waiting for a message the software was never going to send.

AreaWhat worksWhat does not
Reminders and recallsCadence, due list, per-appointment record of every attemptNothing runs on a timer. Somebody presses Send what is due, or nothing goes.
Short-notice listPriority ordering, offer logging, three outcomesNo automatic offers, no first-reply-wins.
BillingInvoices, payments, debtors, end of dayNo card is taken. No payment gateway exists.
ClaimsRecording and trackingNo submission. That needs HICAPS or a fund's channel.
PrescribingFormulary, hand-written interaction checks, signed printed scriptNo eRx token, no pharmacological database.
InventoryStock, movements, reorder flags, purchase ordersNothing is ordered. Purchase orders are recorded, not sent.
CommsTemplates with merge fields, the automated set, the logCampaign sending is not built.
Access controlClinic isolation, five permissions, ownership, full auditNo screen-level separation by job title. A receptionist can open a chart; the audit log is what records that they did.
DataOne database file, backups, owner-only exportNo syncing between machines. The database is not encrypted. Losing the machine loses the practice unless a backup left it.
CredentialsHashed passwords, lockout, optional two-step by emailThe mail app password is stored in plain text. Treat the database file as carrying it.
